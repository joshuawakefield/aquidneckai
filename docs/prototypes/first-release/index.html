Put it to use / Customer work

Follow up without
starting from scratch.

For a self-employed trades owner or a small team. Practice with a fictional estimate request, then decide whether the draft helps.

1. Start with the facts

You run a small carpentry business. A customer has asked for an estimate to repair a wooden garden gate. You need to know whether it opens and closes. No price, visit or completion date has been agreed.

These facts are made up. Keep the exercise fictional; leave out real customer names, contact details and messages.

2. Try this prompt

Using only these fictional facts, draft a friendly follow-up of no more than 80 words. Facts: I run a small carpentry business. A customer asked for an estimate to repair a wooden garden gate. I need to know whether the gate opens and closes before I can prepare the estimate. No price, visit or completion date has been agreed. Ask for the missing detail. Do not invent prices, availability, appointments, guarantees or prior conversations. List uncertain details separately from the message. Leave sending to me.

Paste it into an AI tool you already use. This site does not generate or send a message.

3. Spot the invented promise

Handwritten sample with a deliberate error, not AI output: “Thanks for asking about your garden gate. Does it currently open and close? I can visit on Friday to take a look.”

Which sentence goes beyond the facts?

Reveal the review

Friday was never agreed. Remove the invented visit promise. The checked example below asks only for the missing fact.

This exercise uses handwritten examples. Nothing is generated, saved or sent.

4. Make it yours—or reject it

  • Check every detail against the facts. Remove invented prices, timing or promises.
  • Make the tone sound like you. Decide whether to send a checked message manually.
  • Compare time spent drafting and checking with your usual approach. AI may not help.
What does a checked draft look like?

Handwritten fictional example, not AI output: “Thanks for asking about a garden gate repair estimate. Before I prepare it, could you let me know whether the gate currently opens and closes?”

No price or appointment is promised. The question addresses the missing information; it still needs your review.

A small, useful test.

You need: the made-up facts, an existing AI tool and your judgment.

Success looks like: a factual draft you understand and can check.

Unknown: whether it saves you time. Count checking and editing, too.

No customer upload. No message sending. No financial promises.

What changed?

A real story would begin with a specific, dated development and the original supporting source. A tool’s claim would be attributed to its maker, rather than presented as an independently proven result.

Why it could matter here

For a resident studying a new topic, an educator planning a lesson, or an owner organizing job notes, better notes could be worth testing. That is an editorial hypothesis; an available feature does not establish time saved or improved customer outcomes.

A small next step

Try a made-up job note. Ask the tool to extract only explicitly stated tasks, dates and missing details. Check it line by line before considering any use with real information.

What we don’t know

Accuracy, privacy terms, availability, full price and the amount of checking required would all need verification for a named product. No product recommendation is made in this example.

What would support a published story?

An accessible original source, publication date, exact supporting evidence, a clear distinction between fact and interpretation, and an editorial decision. Corrections and withdrawal history remain available.

The technical part

What “autonomous” means here.

Different jobs, different gates.
One word should not hide the details.

01 / Development

AI-assisted, increasingly autonomous

AI agents can work autonomously within bounded research, code and test tasks, then prepare reviewable changes. Models may vary by task; the developer model is separate from the model used by the site itself. This project does not have one permanent “builder model.”

Completed changes are tested and saved at reviewable checkpoints; interrupted work is reconciled before continuing. The owner must approve a production release; development tasks do not self-deploy. Deployment stays manual.

Longer-term goal: automate more of source discovery and useful-technology monitoring, with safeguards for evidence, rights and human judgment. Broad live discovery is still a goal, not a current service feature.

02 / Content pipeline

Collection is not publication

The existing service checks its configured sources and can use a model to assess new or changed material. Ordinary article candidates wait for human editorial review before publication. A narrow rule for verified official calendar items is separate; it is not a general automatic news-publishing rule.

A source being listed, fetched or assessed does not make its claims true or mean an article has been published.
